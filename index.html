Emergency Medical Literacy

Heat Exhaustion vs. Heatstroke First-Aid Guide

Distinguishing early heat exhaustion from life-threatening heatstroke prevents irreversible organ failure. Toggle between conditions below and review the 5-step rapid cooling protocol.

5-Step Rapid Cooling Protocol

Golden 15 Mins
  1. 1 Relocate to Shade: Immediately move the victim out of direct sun into a ventilated, shaded, or air-conditioned room.
  2. 2 Loosen Outer Layers: Remove excess or tight clothing and elevate legs slightly to improve blood flow.
  3. 3 Target Major Pulse Points: Apply cold wet towels or ice packs to the neck, armpits, and groin where major blood vessels lie close to the skin.
  4. 4 Evaporative Fanning: Spray or sponge cool water onto the skin while fanning vigorously to accelerate heat loss.
  5. 5 Hydrate Only If Alert / Call 1122: Give slow sips of ORS if fully conscious; if confused or unconscious, give nothing by mouth and call Rescue 1122 immediately.

Evidence-Based Public Awareness

Heatwave Myths vs. Medical Facts

Debunking widespread misconceptions in Pakistan that can worsen dehydration or delay life-saving heatstroke care.

Medical Fact: Gulping freezing ice water immediately after intense heat exposure can trigger stomach cramps and constrict blood vessels, slowing heat dissipation. Instead, sit in the shade for 5 minutes and drink cool or room-temperature water mixed with WHO ORS in steady sips.
Medical Fact: Peak ambient temperatures and ground thermal radiation in Pakistan actually occur between 11:00 AM and 4:00 PM, as concrete roads and buildings continue radiating trapped heat well into the late afternoon.
Medical Fact: Classic non-exertional heatstroke frequently affects elderly citizens, infants, and people with chronic illnesses inside poorly ventilated, uninsulated concrete rooms during prolonged power outages or high-humidity nights.
Critical Safety Fact: Never give water or fluids by mouth to a person who is unconscious, drowsy, or vomiting—it can enter their airway and cause fatal choking. Focus on external rapid cooling (wet towels/ice packs on neck, armpits, groin) and call Rescue 1122 immediately.
